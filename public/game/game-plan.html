D6X8 Market: agreed direction and development checklist

October 12, 2026 · A warm, low-poly first-person store simulation with an optional visible owner. The agreed concept image guides the style; it is not an exact gameplay screenshot.

Priority: make running one store feel complete and dependable before enlarging the city or adding paid features. “Implemented” means present in the code and covered by relevant logic checks. Browser visuals, mobile performance, and extended player testing remain unverified.

1. Art and atmosphere

  • Warm cream walls, muted green shop signs, brown wood shelving, pale tiled floors, restrained reflections.
  • Consistent low-poly human proportions, angular faces and hair, simple varied clothing and readable expressions.
  • Products, trees, trucks, bikes, and neighboring buildings follow the same style.
  • Compact checkout at ordinary counter height with the display facing the owner.
  • A short, skippable opening cinematic with store introduction and sound toggle.

3. Neighborhood

  • Furnished houses and open shops, sidewalks, crossings, trees, benches, mailboxes, parked bikes, passing traffic and visible drivers. The owner has a dedicated parked D6X8 car. Sedans, hatchbacks, SUVs and pickups circulate automatically, with new colors, speeds and arrival delays on successive trips. The owner car is driveable: F enters/exits, WASD accelerates/reverses/steers, and the car must stop before exiting.
  • Pedestrians and cyclists; varied customer arrival times, walking speeds, browsing times, moods and payment types.
  • Occasional scripted conversations or fights. These are ambient scenes, not a combat system.
  • Return-to-store button, generated neighborhood streaming with at most nine active outer chunks, enterable ground-floor rooms, and wall/furniture collisions.
  • Added: more than 50 extra procedural buildings, level-gated passive property purchases, eight store levels, a paid all-round store manager, a phone, a procedural TV information channel, and ambient dogs/cats. Added in this update: furnished ground-floor interiors, enterable mall and club, property resale at 70%, family NPCs, police patrols, and neighborhood streaming. Upper-floor access, richer staff animations, full vehicle physics, and day/night cycles remain future work.

4. Paid employee and progression

Hire one manager for $100 in game, then pay $6 for each active minute while the store is open. The manager handles checkout, stocks delivered boxes and reorders low stock when money is available. Unpaid wages pause the manager until enough money is available. Earned balance and customer milestones unlock levels up to 8. City properties can be visited, bought for simulated rent, and sold for 70% of purchase cost. Apartments currently expose furnished ground-floor rooms only.

5. Bug and quality checklist

  • Confirm door, wall, shelf, house and counter collision boundaries.
  • Test camera recovery near walls and correct owner visibility in each view.
  • Verify cinematic completion and skipping, mouse capture/release, focus loss and phone control release.
  • Verify one-minute delivery arrival, box-by-box unloading, driver return, departure and interruption recovery.
  • Verify costs, capacity, partial boxes, price updates, empty shelves, refused purchases and customer inventory returns.
  • Verify every checkout step, incorrect change, wrong payment choice, insufficient scans and repeat clicks.
  • Keep textures and labels from updating when unchanged; clean up removed NPC resources; bound internal event storage.
  • Batch static graphics, preserve dynamic objects, provide a performance setting and test frame rate on real desktop/phone hardware.
  • Long-session and visual browser testing are still required. Zero glitches cannot be guaranteed from code-only tests.

5. Research used

The published Supermarket Simulator description supports stocking, pricing, payments and expansion as the main player loop. Three.js guidance on optimizing many objects and resource disposal informs the performance fixes.

Environment update

  • A 20-minute active-play day cycles through morning, midday, evening and night. Time stops when the browser tab is hidden.
  • Clear, cloudy and rainy weather rotate on the simulation clock. Rain particles are limited and suppressed in indoor rooms; rain sounds follow the sound toggle.
  • Traffic signals control autonomous main-street cars. Cars wait at stop lines and maintain spacing; a pedestrian uses the WALK phase.
  • Street lighting and vehicle headlights brighten at night. Rain darkens roads, and the player car has a night headlamp.
  • Customer shopping bags, phone receipts, cash-drawer indication, recycling and street bins add store/city detail.
  • Traffic routing and weather remain simplified. Visual browser and device performance testing are still required.

Staff and civic expansion

  • Hire one worker per role: cashier, stocker, buyer, pricing manager, general manager. Each role has a separate hiring cost and per-active-minute wage. General manager covers all jobs.
  • Bank cash can be deposited/withdrawn as fictional savings. Peak combined balance unlocks persistent business levels at $1,000, $5,000, $10,000, $25,000 and $50,000.
  • Renovations cost $500 and then $1,500, each completing after 60 active seconds. The second adds outside stairs and an upper stock deck. Builder animations remain simplified.
  • Doors open automatically near the player/customers and close afterward. The player car has an entry/exit door animation.
  • A bank, police station, patrol cars, planes and train provide civic scenery/activity. Transit is ambient; police do not implement a crime/chase system.
  • Juice, bread and eggs join the product catalog. Staff may order low stock automatically.

6. Release boundary

This is a private browser prototype with fictional money. No purchases, ads, shared economy or finished app-store package. Shared player presence is available as an initial multiplayer feature. Preserve the chosen visual direction, stabilize the shop loop, test with players, then decide on expansions and monetization.

Phone and district population update

  • Phone uses a narrow handset layout with home apps, a city destination guide, and a fictional stock exchange. Four companies have simulation-clock quotes; whole-share buy/sell trades use game cash and preserve holdings on reload.
  • 36 pooled citizens follow sidewalks across nearby central and generated neighborhoods; Performance mode shows up to 18. Population is bounded to control cost; these are NPCs, not other players.
  • Existing lots now include two schools, two more clubs, a district police station and a community center with matching ground-floor furnishings. Riverside Sports Ground provides an open recreation area.
  • Online and payment status are clearly shown in the phone. No real ads, checkout, public download or online economy is connected. Opt-in shared player presence uses signed-in Site identity and a D1-backed endpoint.
  • Full upper floors/stairs in every building and realistic school/club activities remain unfinished. Mocked rendering tests cannot confirm graphics or device frame rate.

Shared player presence

Phone → Online & purchases → Join shared city. Other authorized signed-in visitors who join appear as named avatars. Updates arrive every 2.5 seconds, disconnected visitors expire after 20 seconds, and stale records are removed. Separate browser stores retain independent economies. This is presence multiplayer, without shared NPC state, inventory trading, vehicle replication or shared property ownership. Two-user endpoint behavior passed mocked database tests; actual two-browser gameplay and deployment database operation require live testing. The site remains private.

Civic activity and device controls

  • Each police station has local officers and two marked parked service cars. Two moving patrols cover the shop road and north city road.
  • Hospital has two doctors, a nurse, patients, beds and a parked ambulance. Two schools have six students each plus teachers; students appear in class in daytime and outside at midday recess. Club visitors animate locally.
  • Destination actors are distance-culled; touch devices start with reduced rendering resolution and shadows disabled. High graphics remains available.
  • Mobile menus collapse, controls have at least 44px targets, safe-area padding supports notches, and short landscape screens hide secondary HUD blocks. Large screens have scaled HUD text.
  • Standard gamepads: left stick movement, right stick look, A interact, X car, Y phone. D-pad up/down and A navigate modal controls. Compatibility depends on WebGL and browser Gamepad API. Real iPhone, Android, foldable and TV testing remains outstanding.
  • Real ads and payments still require provider accounts, platform configuration and production verification; this prototype does not collect real money.

Taxi, minimap and launch review

  • Top HUD minimap shows nearby roads, destination buildings, citizens, traffic, store, player facing direction, connected players and taxi pickup. Updates are throttled to four per second.
  • Phone or menu → Taxi → Call taxi. Requests require being outside near a road, without driving/carrying boxes/upstairs. Taxi arrives after eight active seconds. Walk to its yellow marker and use E or the Taxi app.
  • Driver asks for a destination: own store, owned houses/apartments and all known city destinations. Fare is displayed before confirmation. Cancellation before travel is free. Travel uses a three-second transition, rather than full route driving.
  • Trips deduct once, reject duplicate booking, use safe outdoor drop-offs and preserve paid destination across reload. Taxi arrival, ride state, save recovery and minimap code checks passed.

Staff dismissal and business empire

  • Staff screen supports firing and rehiring each role. Dismissal removes the worker, stops that role’s automation and future wages, and provides no hiring-fee refund.
  • Businesses app offers eight automated game-money ventures: vending, coffee, bakery, laundry, car wash, franchise, warehouse and hotel. Each has a level gate, purchase price, per-active-minute income and up to five upgrade tiers.
  • Business levels now extend to ten, with peak cash+savings milestones through $1,000,000. Levels stay unlocked after spending.
  • Owned properties support three income upgrades; resale refunds only 70% of original purchase cost. Business and upgrade state saves on this device.
  • Owned vending and coffee businesses add visible starter-shop fixtures and a coffee worker. The larger ventures are currently automated portfolio businesses rather than detailed new interiors.
  • Automated wage, duplicate purchase, level gate, upgrade cap, payout and save-recovery checks passed. This expands the fictional economy; real monetization remains unconnected.
